# IFC FY24 Net Income and Total Assets Financial Report
## IFC FY24 Net Income
### Overview of Net Income
For the fiscal year ended June 30, 2024 (FY24), the International Finance Corporation (IFC) recorded a **net income of $1,485 million** ($1.485 billion). This performance represents a substantial improvement of **$813 million** over the previous fiscal year, where IFC reported a net income of **$672 million** (FY23). In contrast, FY22 had recorded a net loss of **$(464) million** (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, pages 5, 44, 65*).

### Key Drivers of Net Income Performance
According to the financial statements, the significant growth in net income was principally driven by the following factors:
*   **Net Treasury Income:** Treasury income experienced a major increase of **$619 million**, which served as a primary driver of the bottom-line growth (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, page 44*). This was supported by overall higher yields on liquid assets (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, page 5*).
*   **Strong Income from Loans and Debt Securities:** Total income from loans and debt securities (net of allocated charges on borrowings) contributed an increase of **$298 million** (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, page 44*). Specifically, income from debt securities and associated derivatives grew by $293 million, rising from $518 million in FY23 to $811 million in FY24, fueled by a $348 million increase in interest income from higher outstanding balances (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, page 47*).
*   **Designated Income Trends:** Income Available for Designations grew in tandem with net income, totaling **$1,558 million** in FY24 compared to **$681 million** in FY23 (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, page 5*). Starting in FY24, Post-retirement Contribution Reserve Fund (PCRF) income has been excluded from this designation metric (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, page 5*).

## IFC FY24 Total Assets
### Overview of Total Assets
As of June 30, 2024, IFC’s **total assets stood at $108,187 million** ($108.187 billion). This reflects a modest decrease of **$2,360 million (approximately 2.1%)** compared to June 30, 2023, when total assets were **$110,547 million** ($110.547 billion) (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, pages 9, 63*).

### Portfolio Composition & Major Movements
Despite the decrease in overall asset size, IFC's core investment portfolio experienced robust growth, which was offset by significant contractions in liquid assets and derivatives:

*   **Total Investments:** Core investments grew by 14% to **$58,747 million** in FY24, up from **$51,502 million** in FY23. This growth was distributed across all primary investment vehicles (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, pages 9, 63*):
    *   *Loans:* Increased to **$36,437 million** from $31,414 million in FY23.
    *   *Debt Securities:* Increased to **$11,189 million** from $9,310 million in FY23.
    *   *Equity Investments:* Increased to **$11,121 million** from $10,778 million in FY23.
*   **Liquid Assets:** Liquid resources were reduced to accommodate investment disbursement and other capital needs (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, page 9*):
    *   *Trading Securities:* Declined to **$28,555 million** from $31,020 million in FY23 (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, page 63*).
    *   *Securities Purchased Under Resale Agreements & Cash Collateral Pledged:* Dropped significantly to **$1,226 million** from $5,192 million in FY23 (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, page 63*).
    *   *Cash and Due from Banks:* Decreased to **$781 million** from $1,051 million in FY23 (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, page 63*).
*   **Derivative Assets:** Derivative assets experienced a downward revaluation, falling to **$2,954 million** in FY24 from **$5,722 million** in FY23 (*ifc-annual-report-2024-financials.pdf, pages 63, 120*).

The balance sheet structure demonstrates that IFC successfully transitioned more capital into its developmental investment portfolio, funding this expansion primarily through its cash and liquid security buffers.
